6. What other motion engines do (research, 2026-10-24)

None of these projects fixes these problems with a better pixel threshold. They add blob size, persistence or travel over time, and a cut-off for a large sudden change. Some then run a person/vehicle classifier.

  • Frigate (MIT).
    • Motion: a running-average background, contour area, and a recalibration above 80% change (source).
    • Motion only starts object detection. An object is in a zone when the bottom centre of its box is inside, for 3 pictures.
  • Motion (GPL-2): a despeckle filter, a light-switch mute for 5 pictures, and a minimum count of pictures with motion.
  • ZoneMinder (GPL-2): blob filters with minimum and maximum size and count, and a mute after an overload.
  • Blue Iris (proprietary): object size and travel, a reset for large changes, and masks. Its users report no full fix for headlights.
  • Agent DVR: object tracking with a minimum travel and time.
  • Insects. SecuritySpy leaves them to its classifier. Its best advice is an infrared light away from the lens.
  • Classifiers on a Pi 4 CPU (benchmark, YOLO on NCNN):
ModelPi 4 CPUCoral (USB 3)
MobileNet SSD83–113 ms per picture15–18 ms
YOLOX-nano7 pictures a second

A classifier needs a crop of the full picture, plus numpy and a runtime (tflite-runtime 2.3 MB or onnxruntime 21 MB).

The research ranked these for AnyCam, cheapest first:

  1. Blobs (connected changed cells) with an absolute minimum size, and zone membership by the blob's bottom centre.
  2. A tracker that confirms an object over 3+ pictures and a minimum travel, across zones.
  3. A light hold that ends when the picture is stable, not after a fixed 2 s.
  4. A brightness-direction test (headlights, insects).
  5. A classifier on a full-resolution crop, only while motion is seen.
  6. numpy for the cell comparison, which makes 1–4 cheap.

The costs at 128 x 96 are estimates, not measurements.

7. A headlight rule, replayed on the sample clips (C30)

The rule is checked on each comparison, about 4 a second. Each comparison sets the picture against the one 1 s older, on the 128 x 96 grid.

  • Start a pause: all four must hold.
    • 0.5% of the picture or more changed.
    • 90% or more of the changed cells got brighter.
    • They got brighter by +25 grey levels or more on average.
    • In the 1.5 s before, no comparison changed 0.3% or more, unless it was itself 75% or more brighter (a light that grows).
  • During the pause: no recording starts. The slow-movement check drops its older pictures (B73).
  • End of the pause: after two quiet comparisons in a row (under 0.3%), or 4 s after it started.
  • The beam leaving: within 3 s after a pause, a change whose cells are 90% or more darker, by −25 or more, pauses again, at most 6 s after the first start. A darker-only change never starts a pause by itself.

Two versions failed first, and the final rule is shaped by them:

  • A darker-only start fired when the man, in a dark jacket, first walked into the picture.
  • A brighter-only change also fired when he went in the door and the bright doorway reappeared behind him.

The quiet-before condition separates these: a headlight comes out of a quiet picture, a person does not.

Limits.

  • The clips start about 3 s before their trigger, so the history before that counts as quiet.
  • The replay is not the add-on's own code (OpenCV, not Pillow; 7 recorded pictures a second).
  • A person who walks in during a pause is detected when it ends, at most 4 s later. A person who comes and goes within it is missed.

8. The headlight rule on every recording (20–24 Oct)

CrystalHeeler opened the whole front-camera recordings folder for this test: 486 files, 326 recordings (213 by day, 113 at night). The rule of section 7 ran on each recording's first file. It counts as "paused at the trigger" when a pause covers 2.8–4.6 s into the file, where the recording started after its 3–4 s pre-roll. Clips with movement left after the pause were checked frame by frame, and so was a sample of the rest.

Version 3 (section 7):

  • At night it paused at the trigger in 70 of 113 recordings: headlight sweeps, cars on the street, and three day-to-night switches (20 Oct 18:24:22, 21 Oct 18:23:39, 23 Oct 18:20:40).
  • By day it paused in 3, all after dark-hours light.

Three findings changed the rule.

  1. Two headlight sweeps were missed (23 Oct 19:48:11, 22 Oct 20:37:12).
    • Just before the beam, something small changed: a shadow swept the lawn as the light came on (1.0%, darker), and the car itself showed on the street at the top (0.65%).
    • Either broke the "quiet before" check, which allowed nothing at or above 0.3%.
    • Change: quiet before now means nothing at or above 1.5% (unless it was itself mostly brighter).
    • Replay: both are caught, plus a car on the street (22 Oct 20:10:28) and a daytime sun-through-cloud false alarm (24 Oct 09:50:38). Nothing with a person changed.
    • The man going in the door still does not start a pause: changes of 4–15% came just before.
  2. The porch light (20 Oct 18:57:58, 21 Oct 19:19:51).
    • A large change (19–23% of the picture), nearly all brighter, as the porch light came on. The camera switched from infrared to colour, and a person came out at once.
    • The rule paused the person's first seconds.
    • Change: no pause when the picture turns from infrared to colour during the change; that is a light someone switched on. The add-on already measures colour for its night state.
    • The day-to-night switch goes the other way (colour to infrared) and still pauses.
  3. The front door opening (23 Oct 20:26:49, 24 Oct 06:36:14).
    • The storm door's glass makes a narrow bright strip at the right edge (+55 to +76), then a person steps out.
    • The rule pauses; the person keeps moving and is recorded when the pause ends, but the file would start about 4 s late and miss the door opening.
    • Change: a recording that starts right after a pause starts its file at the beginning of the pause, so the pause is inside the pre-roll.
    • This makes any wrong pause cost nothing, as long as the movement goes on past it.
    • It needs about 10 s of video held in memory per armed camera instead of 3 s (about 10 MB at 8 Mbit/s).

After the changes:

  • At night, 73 of 113 recordings are paused at the trigger.
  • By day, 1 of 208: the sun coming out from behind a cloud, also a false alarm.

The 40 night recordings left:

  • Mostly small changes (0.1–1% of the picture) at the top: the street and the far lawn. Cars and their lights on the street, many of them a light leaving (darker only, which never starts a pause).
  • A moth near the lens (23 Oct 18:39:12).
  • The cat (23 Oct 05:31:39).
  • A person coming out of the door (21 Oct 19:18:38).

The street ones need the zone edit: keep NeighborSideYard and the top of FrontYard-Right off the street.

Not checked:

  • Every paused recording was not viewed; those with movement left after the pause were, and a sample of the others (all lights).
  • In this replay "turned to colour" was taken from the clip's overall colour, which wrongly lost the dusk headlight clip (22 Oct 18:21:58, colour before and after). The add-on would compare colour before and after the change.

Headlights. The front cameras paused for a passing light 22 times in the night. 10 passes made no recording. 12 passes made the 29 files. The pause starts at the right moment; the recordings come from what happens around it:

  1. The pause ends while the light is still moving (20 files). A pause lasts at most 4 s (6 s in all). A car's light crosses the front yard in about 10 s. The recording starts 1–3 s after the pause ends:
    • 14 of the 20 through the slow-movement check. It kept storing pictures during the pause, so 5 s later it compared the scene with a lit picture.
    • 6 through the normal checks, on the light still moving (no new pause: the picture before was not quiet).
  2. The zone fires before the pause sees the light (4 files). The first, faint edge of the light passes a zone's level (0.8–1.2% of a zone) before it is 0.5% of the picture. The pause starts in the same second, after the recording.
  3. One picture records at once (3 files, ch6, no zones). A change of 3% or more in one picture starts a recording on the next picture. The log then says "changed in one picture only — not recorded", which is wrong: it was recorded.

Why the replay of section 8 missed this. It checked only whether a pause covered the trigger. It did not run the slow-movement check or the seconds after the pause.

Insects. The back yard records at 0.8% of the picture at night (sensitivity 90, night +15) and outside its zones. Insects flying in the infrared change 1–3%, over several pictures, so the one-picture rule does not stop them.

10. The 3.9.0-rc2.0 changes, replayed (25 Oct)

The add-on's whole live motion path (_motion_feed, with each camera's zones and sensitivity from the settings copy of 24 Oct 22:42) replayed 409 recordings: every first file of 20–24 Oct on the patio camera (ch7), and every first file of the night of 24–25 Oct. Pictures were decoded once, 4 a second, at the detector's size, with the colour of each picture.

What changed (B84, B85):

  • A light pause lasts up to 8 s (12 s in all), not 4 s (6 s). The light still moving right after a pause continues it.
  • The slow-movement check keeps no picture during a pause, so it never compares the scene with a lit one.
  • A live start waits 1.25 s. It does not start when that time shows a light pause, a light change, the camera switching between infrared and colour (a colour jump of 1.0 or more with one side at 2.5 or less), or a one-sided change that stopped at once (a lamp).
  • The one-picture rule ignores a lit lawn.
