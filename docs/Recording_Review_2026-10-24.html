1. Vehicle headlights (C30) — the largest group

  • Clips: 4 from the left (18:21:58 at dusk in colour, 20:55:25, 21:08:35, 23:24:00) and 1 from the right (05:50:13).
  • How they record. A car turning on the street lights the lawns and rakes the porch posts' shadows across them. The beam reaches FrontYard-Left first, so its zone starts the recording, and in the same second FrontYard-Right, NeighborSideYard, Walkway and Patio change too. The change covers 1–10% of the picture and at most 38% of its 16 areas, far below the 75% light rule.
  • Measured signature. The same comparison as the add-on was used: 128 x 96 grey, normalised, each picture against the one 1 s older.
    • At the moment each left clip triggered, 90–100% of the changed cells got brighter, by +37 to +46 grey levels.
    • As the beam swept on, the changed cells were mixed (moving shadows). As it left, they were mostly darker.
    • A walking person or a cat is mixed throughout: 36–58% brighter, mean change about ±10.
  • Share. 14 of the 25 night recordings in the log started in FrontYard-Left with FrontYard-Right passing within 2.5 s, the pattern of every left headlight clip. A person cannot be on both lawns, either side of the walkway, in the same second.
  • The right-side clip (05:50:13) is the car itself passing on the street at the top right: about 1% of the picture, mixed brighter and darker. A brightness test does not catch it. The NeighborSideYard zone reaching the street is the likely cause.
  • No project found by the research has a brighten-only test (section 6). It needs a field test.

3. Insects (B76, B78)

  • Clip 19:48:59 (FrontYard-Right, 10 s): a faint, blurred, infrared-lit streak near the bird feeder at the top right, 10–11 cells (0.08% of the picture), in a different place in each picture.
  • Log. At 19:48:54 the fast checks rejected it ("changed in one picture only"). Five seconds later the slow-movement check compared two new pictures with that 5 s old picture, which held the insect. Both "differed", so it recorded. The insect in the old reference picture made the recording, not anything new. This is B78.

4. Objects moving between zones (B75, C31)

  • The man (07:51:42) and the boy (17:40:36). Every zone they walked through passed its threshold: the man in FrontYard-Left 7.0%, Walkway 37.4%, FrontYard-Right 13.8% and Patio 43.5%. Once a recording runs, a pass in any zone keeps it going, so it stays one file, named after the first zone only. Detection worked; the name hides it.
  • The boy far away (17:42:11–17:43:28). At the tree, FrontYard-Right saw 0.3–0.9% against 1.2% needed, five times. A small, distant figure is too small a share of a large zone, and the slider cannot fix it: at 100, FrontYard-Right still needs 1.0% by day. A smaller zone around the far lawn would (the same figure is a larger share of a smaller zone).
  • The cat (05:31:39). It walked down the Walkway. At 05:31:36 the Walkway saw 4.4% against 4.6% needed, and at 05:31:33 1.7%. FrontYard-Left then recorded it at 1.9% against 0.8%. No other zone saw it before.
    • Walkway at 51 or more would have caught the 4.4%.
    • At 60 it needs 2.97% at night and 5.69% by day; the boy on the walkway by day peaked at 11–22%.
    • Catching the farther 1.7% sighting needs 73.
  • Two zones in the same second (B75). A recording needs a second changed picture in the same zone within 0.5 s. An object that passes one zone, then the next, confirms neither. The log shows this 4 times (19:50:16, 21:52:16, 22:13:05, 00:07:02).

6. What other motion engines do (research, 2026-10-24)

None of these projects fixes these problems with a better pixel threshold. They add blob size, persistence or travel over time, and a cut-off for a large sudden change. Some then run a person/vehicle classifier.

  • Frigate (MIT).
    • Motion: a running-average background, contour area, and a recalibration above 80% change (source).
    • Motion only starts object detection. An object is in a zone when the bottom centre of its box is inside, for 3 pictures.
  • Motion (GPL-2): a despeckle filter, a light-switch mute for 5 pictures, and a minimum count of pictures with motion.
  • ZoneMinder (GPL-2): blob filters with minimum and maximum size and count, and a mute after an overload.
  • Blue Iris (proprietary): object size and travel, a reset for large changes, and masks. Its users report no full fix for headlights.
  • Agent DVR: object tracking with a minimum travel and time.
  • Insects. SecuritySpy leaves them to its classifier. Its best advice is an infrared light away from the lens.
  • Classifiers on a Pi 4 CPU (benchmark, YOLO on NCNN):

A classifier needs a crop of the full picture, plus numpy and a runtime (tflite-runtime 2.3 MB or onnxruntime 21 MB).

The research ranked these for AnyCam, cheapest first:

  1. Blobs (connected changed cells) with an absolute minimum size, and zone membership by the blob's bottom centre.
  2. A tracker that confirms an object over 3+ pictures and a minimum travel, across zones.
  3. A light hold that ends when the picture is stable, not after a fixed 2 s.
  4. A brightness-direction test (headlights, insects).
  5. A classifier on a full-resolution crop, only while motion is seen.
  6. numpy for the cell comparison, which makes 1–4 cheap.

The costs at 128 x 96 are estimates, not measurements.

7. A headlight rule, replayed on the sample clips (C30)

The rule is checked on each comparison, about 4 a second. Each comparison sets the picture against the one 1 s older, on the 128 x 96 grid.

  • Start a pause: all four must hold.
    • 0.5% of the picture or more changed.
    • 90% or more of the changed cells got brighter.
    • They got brighter by +25 grey levels or more on average.
    • In the 1.5 s before, no comparison changed 0.3% or more, unless it was itself 75% or more brighter (a light that grows).
  • During the pause: no recording starts. The slow-movement check drops its older pictures (B73).
  • End of the pause: after two quiet comparisons in a row (under 0.3%), or 4 s after it started.
  • The beam leaving: within 3 s after a pause, a change whose cells are 90% or more darker, by −25 or more, pauses again, at most 6 s after the first start. A darker-only change never starts a pause by itself.

Two versions failed first, and the final rule is shaped by them:

  • A darker-only start fired when the man, in a dark jacket, first walked into the picture.
  • A brighter-only change also fired when he went in the door and the bright doorway reappeared behind him.

The quiet-before condition separates these: a headlight comes out of a quiet picture, a person does not.

Limits.

  • The clips start about 3 s before their trigger, so the history before that counts as quiet.
  • The replay is not the add-on's own code (OpenCV, not Pillow; 7 recorded pictures a second).
  • A person who walks in during a pause is detected when it ends, at most 4 s later. A person who comes and goes within it is missed.
