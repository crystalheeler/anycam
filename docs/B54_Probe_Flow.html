AnyCam Probe Flow

Every request each camera would receive under the proposed B54 design, from the first network scan to the last path of a full walk. One chart per camera. Proposed, not built.

A hit is one request the camera itself receives. Multicast queries go to the whole network at once, and a camera only answers them; they are shown but not counted as hits. Requests on one RTSP connection are counted one by one.

Every column also includes 1 ARP request and 54 TCP port checks, the same for every device on the network; the totals above leave them out. "Today" is worked out from the scan code's own path tables and the field logs: for each extra open web port the scan currently tries 17 MJPEG, 11 HLS, 9 WebRTC and 7 WebSocket paths (44 requests), plus up to 11 identity pages. Password-entry totals count a stream check (ffprobe) as about 5 requests. The Lorex DVR's scan today expands a channel list per DVR recipe, which these tables cannot count reliably.

Amcrest camera (test system C)

Seen in the field: RTSP on 554, Dahua's admin port 37777 (the second card of B33). assumed ONVIF on port 80 with a login, and 2 ONVIF profiles (main and sub), as Amcrest ships them.
